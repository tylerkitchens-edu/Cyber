Mobile Devices and Printers

Lesson

Learning Objectives

  • Name the laptop parts a technician commonly replaces.
  • Connect a phone or laptop with Bluetooth, NFC, a hotspot or tethering, and know when each fits.
  • Explain how organizations secure and manage mobile devices (screen locks, MDM, remote wipe).
  • Tell laser, inkjet, thermal, impact and 3D printers apart, and put the laser printing steps in order.
  • Install, share and secure a printer, and fix the most common printer problems.

Laptops: what a technician replaces

Laptops pack the same parts as a desktop into a small case, so many parts are built in. The ones a technician usually replaces:

  • Battery: wears out after a few hundred charge cycles. A swollen battery is a safety hazard: stop using the laptop and replace it.
  • Memory (RAM): SO-DIMM modules, smaller than desktop DIMMs. Many thin laptops have RAM soldered to the motherboard, so it cannot be upgraded.
  • Storage: usually an M.2 SSD (see the Storage types lesson); older laptops use a 2.5-inch drive.
  • Keyboard, touchpad, screen and wireless card: replaced as whole parts. The Wi-Fi antenna wires run up through the hinge into the screen lid.

Before opening any laptop: unplug it, disconnect the battery if you can, ground yourself against static electricity, and keep the tiny screws organized.

Connecting mobile devices

  • Bluetooth: short-range wireless for headsets, keyboards and other accessories. Devices are paired once: make one discoverable, select it on the other, and confirm the matching code.
  • NFC (near-field communication): works only within a few centimetres. Used for tap-to-pay and tapping a badge or phone to a reader.
  • Hotspot: the phone shares its cellular data connection over Wi-Fi, so several devices can connect.
  • Tethering: shares the phone's connection with one device, over USB or Bluetooth.
  • Cellular: 4G LTE and 5G data from the mobile carrier. Phones and some laptops also have GPS for location.
  • Wired and sync: USB-C (Lightning on older iPhones) for charging and data. Contacts, calendars, photos and email usually sync through a cloud account rather than a cable.

A field technician's laptop has no Wi-Fi at a customer site, but their work phone has a good 5G signal. What lets the laptop get online?

A hotspot or tethering shares the phone's cellular data connection with the laptop. NFC only reaches a few centimetres and is used for things like tap-to-pay.

Securing and managing mobile devices

Phones and laptops are easy to lose and carry company email and data, so organizations protect them:

  • Screen lock: PIN, password, fingerprint or face. Set the device to lock itself after a short idle time.
  • Encryption: modern phones encrypt storage once a screen lock is set; laptops use BitLocker (Windows) or FileVault (macOS).
  • Multifactor authentication (MFA): a stolen password alone is not enough to sign in.
  • MDM (mobile device management): software such as Microsoft Intune that lets IT enforce screen locks and encryption, push apps and updates, and locate, lock or remotely wipe a lost device.
  • BYOD (bring your own device): staff use their own phones for work. MDM can keep work apps and data in a separate, managed area, so IT can wipe only the work data and leave personal photos alone.
  • Updates and app sources: keep the OS updated and install apps only from the official app store.

An employee leaves their company phone in a taxi. It holds company email. What should IT do first?

MDM can locate, lock and remotely wipe a managed device, which protects the company data even if the phone is never found.

Print quality is measured in DPI (dots per inch): the higher the number, the finer the detail. Speed is measured in PPM (pages per minute).

In Windows, a printer's Printing Preferences set the paper size and tray, colour or black and white, quality, and the Finishing options: two-sided (duplex) printing, several pages per sheet, collating, and stapling on printers that can staple.

The laser printing process

The A+ exam expects these seven steps in order:

  1. Processing: the printer turns the page into a raster image, a map of every dot on the page.
  2. Charging: a roller (or corona wire) gives the drum a uniform negative charge.
  3. Exposing: the laser writes the image onto the drum, removing the charge wherever toner should go.
  4. Developing: toner sticks to the areas the laser touched.
  5. Transferring: the toner moves from the drum onto the paper.
  6. Fusing: heat and pressure melt the toner into the paper. That is why pages come out warm.
  7. Cleaning: leftover toner is wiped off the drum and the charge is removed, ready for the next page.

Installing, sharing and securing printers

  • Connections: USB for one computer; Ethernet or Wi-Fi to share the printer on the network. Network printers should get a reserved IP address so computers can always find them.
  • Drivers: the driver is the software that lets the operating system talk to that printer model. Get it from the manufacturer or Windows Update.
  • Page description language (PDL): the language that describes how each page should look so the printer can draw it. The two common ones are PCL (from HP) and PostScript (from Adobe).
  • Sharing: a print server, or Windows printer sharing, lets several users print to one printer and lets IT manage the queue.
  • Security: printers are network devices and get attacked too. Change the default admin password, update the firmware, and use secure (badge or PIN) release so confidential pages are not left in the tray. Many printers store scanned and printed jobs on an internal drive: wipe it before the printer is sold or thrown away.

What is a page description language (PDL) such as PCL or PostScript?

The driver translates a document into a PDL such as PCL or PostScript, and the printer uses that description to build the page.

Maintenance and common problems

  • Faded or streaky pages: toner or ink is low, or (inkjet) the print heads need cleaning from the printer's maintenance menu.
  • Toner smears or rubs off: the fuser is not getting hot enough; replace it (usually as part of a maintenance kit).
  • Same mark repeating down the page: a damaged drum or roller.
  • Paper jams: worn pickup rollers, damp paper or the wrong paper type. Clear jams in the direction the paper travels.
  • Garbled characters: the wrong or a corrupt driver; reinstall the correct one.
  • Jobs stuck in the queue: clear the queue and restart the Print Spooler service.

Laser printers need a maintenance kit (fuser, rollers) after a set number of pages; the printer's status page shows the count. After installing it, reset the counter.

Pages from an office laser printer come out with toner that smudges when you run a finger over it. Which part is most likely failing?

The fuser melts toner into the paper. If it does not get hot enough, the toner sits loose on the page and smudges.

Try it on your computer (Windows, 3 minutes)

  1. Open Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Printers & scanners. Which printers are listed? "Microsoft Print to PDF" is a software printer that saves a PDF instead of printing.
  2. Press Win+R, type services.msc and press Enter. Find Print Spooler: this is the service you restart when jobs get stuck in the queue. Look, but do not stop it.
  3. On your phone, find the hotspot setting and the screen lock timeout. How long does your phone stay unlocked when you put it down?
