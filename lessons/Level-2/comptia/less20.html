What the exam expects

Securing a mobile device

  • Screen lock: PIN, password, pattern, fingerprint or face. Set a short auto-lock time.
  • Locator apps and remote wipe: find a lost device, or erase it if it can't be recovered.
  • Updates: install OS and app updates; get apps only from the official stores.
  • Mobile device management (MDM): lets the company enforce policies, push apps and wipe company data. With BYOD (bring your own device), work data is often kept in a separate, managed work profile.
  • Encryption and backups: modern iOS and Android devices encrypt their storage once a screen lock is set. Back up to iCloud or Google.

Troubleshooting mobile problems

SymptomTry first
An app crashes or won't openForce-stop and reopen it; update it; clear its cache (Android); reinstall it
Battery drains fastCheck battery use by app; lower brightness; turn off unused radios; check battery health
Device is slowRestart; close apps; free storage space; update the OS
OS update failsCharge the device, connect to Wi-Fi, free storage space, then retry
Screen won't rotateCheck that rotation lock is off
Won't connect to Wi-Fi or BluetoothToggle airplane mode; forget and rejoin the network; re-pair the device

Signs of a security problem

Unexpected high data use, pop-ups and fake security warnings, apps you didn't install, unknown charges, or a device running hot when idle. Common causes: apps installed from outside the store (sideloading, such as an Android .apk file), a jailbroken (iOS) or rooted (Android) device, or developer mode left on.

Check your understanding

An employee's company phone is stolen and can't be found. What should IT do to protect company data?

A remote wipe erases the data, so the thief can't read it. MDM can send the wipe to a managed device.

A user's Android phone suddenly shows pop-ups and uses a lot of data. They recently installed a game from a website, not the Play Store. What is the most likely cause?

Installing an .apk from outside the official store (sideloading) skips the store's checks. Remove the app and scan the device.

An iPhone update keeps failing. What should the user check first?

Updates need enough free space, a stable connection and enough battery. A factory reset is a last resort.

Try it on your phone (5 minutes)

  1. On your phone, open the battery settings. Which app used the most battery today?
  2. Find the auto-lock (screen timeout) setting. How long is it? 30 seconds to 2 minutes is a sensible choice.
  3. Check that your phone's locator is on: Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android). Look at the remote options it offers: play sound, lock, erase.
