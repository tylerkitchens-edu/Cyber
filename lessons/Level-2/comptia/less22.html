Lesson 13: Implementing Operational Procedures

What the exam expects

Tickets and documentation

  • Ticket: who, what, when, the category and severity, the steps taken, and how it was resolved. Write it so the next technician could repeat your fix.
  • Asset management: an inventory of hardware and software, with asset tags, owners and warranty dates.
  • Documents: standard operating procedures (SOPs), the acceptable use policy (AUP), a knowledge base, incident reports, and onboarding and offboarding checklists.

Change management

Every change is requested and documented with its purpose, scope, risk analysis, test results and a rollback plan. A change advisory board (CAB) approves it, it happens in a planned maintenance window, and end users confirm it works.

Backups

TypeBacks upTo restore you need
FullEverythingThe last full backup
IncrementalChanges since the last backup of any typeThe last full plus every incremental since
DifferentialChanges since the last full backupThe last full plus the latest differential

The 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies of the data, on 2 different kinds of media, with 1 copy off site. Test restores regularly; a backup you can't restore is not a backup.

Safety and the environment

  • Prevent electrostatic discharge (ESD) with an antistatic wrist strap and mat; keep components in antistatic bags.
  • Unplug equipment before opening it; lift with your legs; never open a power supply or a CRT monitor.
  • Recycle batteries, toner and electronics according to local rules, and read the safety data sheet (SDS) for chemicals.

Incidents, licensing and professionalism

  • Incident response: report it, preserve the evidence, and keep a chain of custody that records everyone who handles it.
  • Licensing: follow the end-user license agreement (EULA); personal licenses are not for business use; open-source software has its own license terms.
  • Professionalism: be on time, listen without interrupting, avoid jargon, don't take personal calls, respect the customer's private data, and confirm the problem is solved before you leave.

Check your understanding

A full backup runs on Sunday and an incremental backup every other night. The server fails on Thursday morning. What do you need to restore?

Each incremental only holds the changes since the backup before it, so you need the full backup plus every incremental after it. With differential backups you'd need only the full plus Wednesday's.

A planned server update goes wrong during the maintenance window. Which part of the change request tells the team what to do?

The rollback plan describes how to undo the change and return to the last working state.

You find illegal content on a company laptop during a repair. What must you do?

Follow the incident response process: report it to the right person, don't change the evidence, and record everyone who handles it.

Try it (10 minutes)

  1. Write a practice ticket for a problem you had recently with any device: the symptom, the steps you tried, and the fix. Could someone else follow it?
  2. Plan a 3-2-1 backup for your own files: where are your three copies, on which two kinds of media, and which one is off site (a cloud service counts)?
  3. Windows: search for File History. Is it on, and where does it save?
