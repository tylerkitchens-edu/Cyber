What the exam expects

Signing in: authentication factors

  • Something you know: password, PIN.
  • Something you have: phone authenticator app, hardware token, smart card.
  • Something you are: fingerprint, face (Windows Hello).

Multifactor authentication (MFA) needs two different kinds of factor. A password plus a PIN is still one factor (both are something you know). Single sign-on (SSO) lets one sign-in open many applications.

Workstation good practice

  • Strong passwords, an account lockout after several failed attempts, and a screen lock after a few minutes idle.
  • Rename or disable the built-in Administrator account and disable the Guest account. Use standard accounts day to day.
  • Turn off AutoRun/AutoPlay for USB drives, keep updates and antivirus on, and encrypt the drive (BitLocker).

Browser security

  • Download browsers and extensions only from trusted sources, and remove extensions you don't use.
  • Check for HTTPS and a valid certificate before typing passwords.
  • Use a password manager, keep the pop-up blocker on, and clear the cache and cookies when troubleshooting.

The malware removal steps (in order)

  1. Investigate and verify the malware symptoms.
  2. Quarantine the infected system (disconnect it from the network).
  3. Disable System Restore in Windows (restore points may hold the malware).
  4. Remediate: update the anti-malware software, then scan and remove (Safe Mode or a boot-time scan if needed).
  5. Schedule scans and run updates.
  6. Enable System Restore and create a new restore point.
  7. Educate the end user.

Check your understanding

You have confirmed that a laptop is infected with malware. What is the next step?

After investigating and verifying the symptoms, quarantine the system so the malware can't spread. Disabling System Restore comes next.

Which of these is true multifactor authentication?

The password is something you know and the app code is something you have. The other answers use two factors of the same kind.

Why should System Restore be disabled while removing malware?

Malware can hide in restore points. Turn System Restore back on, with a fresh restore point, once the system is clean.

Try it on your computer (Windows, 5 minutes)

  1. Open Settings > Accounts > Sign-in options. Which Windows Hello options does your computer offer?
  2. Open Windows Security > Virus & threat protection. When was the last scan, and are the protection updates current?
  3. In your browser, open the extensions page (chrome://extensions or edge://extensions). Is there one you don't use? Removing it is good practice.
