Lesson 9: Executing Incident Response Plans

CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-004)

Overview

This lesson is the hands-on side of incident response: triaging, collecting evidence properly, containing the threat, and restoring service, step by step.

You will learn:

  • developing plans and playbooks, defining roles, and running tabletop and simulation exercises;
  • collecting, correlating and enriching logs; triage; building a timeline; judging severity;
  • evidence handling: chain of custody, integrity checks, preservation and legal hold;
  • isolation, escalation, remediation, restoration and root cause analysis.

On the exam this is mostly objective 3.3 (implementing incident response techniques).

Step 3: Key ideas to remember

Before the incident

An incident response plan and a communication plan, playbooks for common incident types, and named roles (incident lead, scribe, communications, technical leads). Teams practise with tabletop exercises (talking through a scenario around a table) and simulations (actually performing the steps in a test environment).

During the incident

  1. Triage: is it real, how bad, what is affected? Collect and correlate logs, and enrich them (asset owner, user, threat intelligence).
  2. Timeline: put events in order across systems (this is why clocks must be synchronized).
  3. Severity, impact and priority decide who is pulled in and how fast.
  4. Gather evidence before it disappears (below).
  5. Isolate affected systems (network isolation through EDR, disabling accounts) and escalate as the plan says.
  6. Remediate and verify, release from isolation, restore from known-good backups, and monitor closely.
  7. Find the root cause and develop corrective actions so it can't happen the same way again.

Handling evidence

  • Order of volatility: collect what disappears first. Memory goes before disk; pulling the power loses everything in RAM.
  • Data integrity: hash evidence (for example SHA-256) when you collect it, and work on copies, so you can prove nothing changed.
  • Chain of custody: a record of who handled the evidence, when, and why.
  • Legal hold: an instruction to preserve relevant data, overriding normal deletion schedules, when litigation is expected.

Check your understanding

A workstation is running live malware. What should be collected before anyone shuts it down?

Memory is the most volatile evidence and often holds the malware, network connections and keys. Isolate the machine from the network instead of powering it off.

Why do investigators hash a disk image when they collect it?

Re-hashing later and getting the same value shows integrity. That, together with chain of custody, makes evidence trustworthy.

The team walks through a ransomware scenario in a meeting room, discussing what each person would do. What is this?

Tabletops are discussion-based. Simulations actually carry out the steps in a test environment.
