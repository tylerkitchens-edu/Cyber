Lesson 2: Applying Risk Management Strategies

CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-004)

Overview

An analyst finds far more problems than anyone can fix at once. Risk management is how an organization decides what to fix first, what to live with, and what to hand to someone else.

You will learn:

  • the kinds of security controls (administrative, technical, physical) and what each one does (prevent, detect, respond, correct);
  • risk appetite, inherent risk and residual risk;
  • the four ways to treat a risk: accept, transfer, avoid, mitigate;
  • risk that comes from suppliers and third-party software.

On the exam this is mostly objective 2.4 (control types, risks and vulnerability management).

Step 2: Key ideas to remember

Controls: what kind, and what they do

Control typeExamples
Administrative (managerial)Policies, training, background checks, change management
TechnicalFirewalls, MFA, encryption, EDR
PhysicalLocks, badges, fences, cameras
Control functionWhat it doesExample
PreventativeStops it happeningMFA blocks a stolen password
DetectiveNotices it happenedAn IDS alert, a log review
ResponsiveActs while it is happeningEDR isolating an infected laptop
CorrectivePuts things right afterwardsRestoring from backup, applying a patch

Risk in plain terms

  • Inherent risk is the risk before any controls. Residual risk is what is left after the controls are in place. There is always some residual risk.
  • Risk appetite is how much risk leadership is willing to live with to get the job done. If residual risk is above the appetite, more must be done.
  • Policies, governance and service-level objectives (for example "critical vulnerabilities patched within 15 days") turn the appetite into rules analysts can follow.

Four ways to treat a risk

  • Mitigate: add controls to reduce it (patch, segment, add MFA).
  • Transfer: make someone else carry the cost (cyber insurance, a contract with a provider).
  • Avoid: stop doing the risky thing (retire the old system, don't launch the feature).
  • Accept: leadership signs off on living with it, usually because fixing it costs more than the risk. Accepted risks are written down and reviewed.

Third-party and supply chain risk

Much of an organization's risk comes from others: cloud providers, managed service providers, and the open-source libraries inside its software. Software composition analysis (SCA) tools list those libraries and flag known vulnerabilities, and a software bill of materials (SBOM) is the ingredient list that lets you answer "are we using the vulnerable library?" quickly.

Check your understanding

A company buys cyber insurance to cover the cost of a ransomware attack. Which risk treatment is this?

Insurance moves the financial impact to someone else. It does not make an attack less likely, so other controls are still needed.

A server is patched, segmented and monitored. The risk that still remains is called:

Inherent risk is before controls; residual risk is what is left after them. Leadership compares residual risk with its risk appetite.

An intrusion detection system raises an alert on suspicious traffic. What is its control function?

An IDS notices and reports; it does not block. (An intrusion prevention system, IPS, would be preventative.)
