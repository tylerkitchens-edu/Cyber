Lesson 6: Threat Intelligence and Threat Hunting

CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-004)

Overview

Threat intelligence tells you who is attacking organizations like yours and how. Threat hunting uses that knowledge to go looking for attackers who got past your alerts.

You will learn:

  • threat actors, their tactics, techniques and procedures (TTPs), and MITRE ATT&CK;
  • the Pyramid of Pain, and why some indicators matter more than others;
  • where intelligence comes from and how to judge it;
  • how a hypothesis-driven threat hunt works, plus threat modeling and attack frameworks.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 1.4 (threat intelligence and threat hunting) and 3.1 (attack methodology frameworks).

Step 3: Key ideas to remember

Know the adversary

  • Advanced persistent threats (APTs) are skilled, well-funded groups, often state-backed, that stay hidden for a long time. Insider threats already have legitimate access.
  • TTPs describe how an attacker works. MITRE ATT&CK is a free catalogue of real attacker tactics (the goal, such as persistence) and techniques (how, such as a scheduled task). A heat map of ATT&CK shows which techniques you can detect and where your gaps are.
  • Attack frameworks describe an intrusion step by step: the Cyber Kill Chain (reconnaissance through actions on objectives), the Diamond Model (adversary, capability, infrastructure, victim) and MITRE ATT&CK.

The Pyramid of Pain

From bottom (easy for the attacker to change) to top (hard): hash values, IP addresses, domain names, network and host artifacts, tools, and TTPs. Blocking a file hash costs an attacker seconds; detecting their behaviour forces them to change how they work.

Judging intelligence

  • Sources: open-source intelligence (OSINT: public reports, feeds), closed-source (paid feeds, government) and sharing communities such as industry ISACs.
  • Good intelligence is timely, relevant and accurate. An old indicator list for an industry you aren't in is low-value.
  • Indicators of compromise (IoCs) are atomic (one value, such as an IP or hash) or behavioural (a pattern, such as Word starting PowerShell).

Threat hunting, modeling and deception

  • A hunt starts with a hypothesis ("an attacker is using scheduled tasks for persistence"), searches the data, and turns what it finds into new detections.
  • Threat modeling looks for weaknesses in a design. STRIDE stands for Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service and Elevation of privilege.
  • Cyber deception (honeypots, honeytokens, fake credentials) produces high-confidence alerts, because no legitimate user should ever touch them.

Check your understanding

According to the Pyramid of Pain, which detection forces an attacker to change the most?

Hashes, IPs and domains are cheap to change. Changing how they operate is expensive, so TTP-based detection hurts most.

How does a threat hunt normally start?

Hunting is proactive: it looks for what the alerts missed, starting from a testable hypothesis.

A threat feed lists a single IP address used by a ransomware group. What kind of indicator is that?

Atomic indicators are single values (IP, domain, hash). Behavioural indicators describe patterns of activity.
