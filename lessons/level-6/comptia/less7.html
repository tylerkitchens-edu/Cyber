Lesson 7: Assessing Network Vulnerabilities

CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-004)

Overview

Vulnerability management is a large part of the CySA+ exam. This lesson covers how to plan and run scans, read their output, and decide what to fix first.

You will learn:

  • scan types and how to plan scans without disrupting the business;
  • the main scanning and assessment tools and what their output means;
  • prioritising with CVSS, EPSS, active exploitation and asset context;
  • mitigation options, and checking that a fix actually worked.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 2.1 (scanning methods), 2.2 (assessment tool output) and 2.3 (prioritising and mitigating).

Step 3: Key ideas to remember

Planning a scan

  • Start from an accurate asset inventory: you can't scan what you don't know about.
  • Plan around the business: schedule outside busy hours, watch performance on fragile or sensitive systems, account for network segmentation, and meet regulatory requirements (for example PCI DSS for card data).
Scan choiceWhat it means
Internal vs externalFrom inside the network, or from the internet as an attacker would see you
Credentialed vs non-credentialedSigned in to the system (sees missing patches and settings; more accurate) or not (sees only what is exposed)
Agent vs agentlessSoftware on each host reports in (good for laptops that roam) or the scanner reaches out over the network
Active vs passiveSends probes, or only listens to traffic (safer for OT)
DiscoveryMapping scans and device fingerprinting to find what exists
BaselineChecks settings against a standard such as CIS Benchmarks

Tools you should recognise

Network mapping: Nmap, Angry IP Scanner, Masscan. Vulnerability scanners: Nessus, OpenVAS, Nuclei. Web application scanners: Burp Suite, ZAP, Nikto. Cloud and infrastructure-as-code checks: ScoutSuite, Prowler, Trivy, Checkov. Multipurpose and reconnaissance: Metasploit, Maltego, Recon-ng. Breach and attack simulation: Atomic Red Team, Caldera.

Deciding what to fix first

  • CVSS scores how severe a vulnerability is (0-10). EPSS estimates how likely it is to be exploited in the next 30 days.
  • Raise the priority when it is actively exploited (for example on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list), when the asset is valuable or internet-facing, and when there is an easy exploit. Lower it for isolated systems.
  • Check findings for false positives (reported but not really there) before asking a team to fix them. False negatives (real but missed) are why credentialed scans matter.
  • Mitigations: patch, change the configuration, add a compensating control (such as segmentation or a WAF) when you can't patch, or record a formal exception. Then validate by rescanning.

Check your understanding

Which scan finds the most missing patches on a Windows server?

Signed in, the scanner can read installed software versions and settings instead of guessing from the outside.

Finding A: CVSS 9.8 on an isolated lab machine with no known exploits. Finding B: CVSS 7.5 on an internet-facing server, on CISA's Known Exploited list. Which first?

Severity alone isn't priority. Exposure, active exploitation and asset value push B ahead.

A server can't be patched until a vendor update arrives next quarter. What is the best interim step?

Compensating controls reduce the risk until the real fix is possible; the exception documents the decision and its review date.
