Lesson 8: Managing Incident Response and Communication

CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-004)

Overview

When an incident is declared, the technical work is only half the job. The rest is telling the right people the right things at the right time, and reporting clearly afterwards.

You will learn:

  • the phases of the incident response process;
  • incident declaration, escalation and communication plans;
  • who needs to be told (legal, PR, regulators, law enforcement, customers) and how;
  • reporting: executive summaries, lessons learned, metrics and handovers, for both incidents and vulnerability management.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 3.2 (incident response process), 4.1 (vulnerability management reporting) and 4.2 (incident response reporting and communication).

Step 3: Key ideas to remember

The incident response process

CySA+ uses these phases: Preparation (plans, tools, training) → Detection → Analysis → Containment (stop the spread) → Eradication (remove the cause) → Recovery (restore and monitor) → Post-incident (lessons learned). NIST groups the same work into four phases, so you may see both.

Communication

  • Declare and escalate by the plan's criteria, so the right people are pulled in at the right severity.
  • The communication plan names the stakeholders and who speaks to whom: leadership, the legal team (notification duties, evidence), public relations (all public statements), regulators (often with deadlines), law enforcement and customers.
  • Use out-of-band channels (a phone bridge or a separate chat system) if the attacker might be reading company email or chat.
  • Keep a clean shift or incident handover: what's known, what's done, what's next.

Reports and metrics

  • An executive summary states the impact and decisions needed in plain language, without technical detail.
  • The after-action report covers the timeline, root cause, what worked, what didn't, and corrective actions.
  • Incident metrics: mean time to detect (MTTD), to respond, and to remediate, alert volume, and false-positive rate. Vulnerability metrics: open findings by risk, trends, SLA compliance, and the inhibitors to remediation (legacy systems, business disruption, vendor contracts, no patch available).

Check your understanding

The attacker may have access to company email. How should the response team coordinate?

If the attacker can read normal channels, they'll see your plans. Out-of-band communication protects the response.

Who should approve a public statement about a breach?

Public statements carry legal and reputational weight, so PR and legal own them under the communication plan.

Which metric measures how long it takes, on average, to notice an incident after it starts?

MTTD measures detection speed. Mean time to respond and to remediate measure what happens after detection.
