Lesson 12: Explain Incident Response and Monitoring Concepts

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801)

Overview

Sooner or later, something gets through. This lesson covers how security teams watch for attacks, how they respond when one happens, and how automation and AI help.

You will learn:

  • monitoring: log aggregation, SIEM, alerting and alert tuning, dashboards, agents and agentless tools;
  • the incident response process: preparation, identification, investigation, containment, eradication and recovery, notification and lessons learned;
  • digital forensics basics: chain of custody, preservation, legal hold and e-discovery;
  • automation and orchestration (SOAR, scripting) and how AI is used in security operations, new in SY0-801.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 4.4 (alerting and monitoring), 4.6 (automation and orchestration, including AI) and 4.7 (incident response).

Step 3: Key ideas to remember

Monitoring

  • A SIEM (security information and event management) collects logs from everywhere (log aggregation), links related events and raises alerts.
  • Alert tuning cuts false positives so analysts don't drown in noise, while making sure real attacks still alert.
  • Collection can use an agent installed on each system or be agentless (pulling logs remotely). Logs are archived for as long as policy or law requires.
  • SCAP (Security Content Automation Protocol) lets tools check systems against benchmarks automatically.

The incident response process

PhaseWhat happens
PreparationPlaybooks, roles, training, tabletop exercises and simulations, before anything happens
IdentificationDetect and confirm the incident (alerts, advisories, threat hunting)
InvestigationForensics: what happened, how, and how far it spread
ContainmentStop the spread: quarantine or isolate affected systems
Eradication and recoveryRemove the attacker and malware, restore from clean backups, monitor closely
NotificationTell stakeholders, customers, regulators and law enforcement as required
Post-incidentRoot cause analysis, lessons learned and a written report

During a ransomware attack, some organizations also go through negotiation; this is a business and legal decision, never one for the analyst alone.

Handling evidence

Chain of custody records everyone who handled each piece of evidence, so it holds up in court. Preservation means capturing evidence before it changes (memory first, as it is lost at power-off). A legal hold stops data from being deleted; e-discovery finds and hands over electronic records for legal cases.

Automation, orchestration and AI

  • Scripts and SOAR (security orchestration, automation and response) handle repetitive work: provisioning users, creating tickets, enriching alerts, enforcing baselines, isolating a device.
  • Benefits: speed, consistency, fewer mistakes, staff freed for harder work. Risks: complexity, cost, and one bad rule acting at machine speed. Build in guardrails.
  • AI now helps with chatbots for analysts, predictive analysis, AI-adjusted baselines for anomaly detection, and agentic AI that can take actions itself, which needs the strictest guardrails.

Check your understanding

Malware is spreading from one infected laptop. The analyst disconnects it from the network. Which phase is this?

Isolating the device stops the spread. Removing the malware afterwards is eradication.

Why must a chain of custody be kept for a hard drive taken as evidence?

The record of who handled it and when proves its integrity.

A SOC gets 5,000 alerts a day, almost all false alarms, and analysts have started ignoring them. What is the first fix?

Alert tuning reduces noise so real alerts get attention. Alert fatigue is a real risk.
