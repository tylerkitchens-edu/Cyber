Lesson 14: Summarize Security Governance Concepts

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801)

Overview

Governance is how an organization decides and writes down how security should work, and checks that it does. Technical controls only work when policies, standards and procedures back them up.

You will learn:

  • the hierarchy of policies, standards, procedures and guidelines, and the common policies (acceptable use, BYOD, incident response, data classification and more);
  • business continuity and disaster recovery plans as governance documents;
  • audits and assessments: internal and external, gap analysis, and the frameworks they measure against;
  • security awareness training: types, delivery and measuring whether it works.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 5.1 (governance artifacts), 5.5 (audits and assessments) and 5.6 (security awareness).

Step 2: Key ideas to remember

The document hierarchy

DocumentSaysExample
PolicyWhat must happen, and why (approved by leadership)"All company data must be classified."
StandardThe specific, measurable requirement"Passwords are at least 14 characters"; "Encrypt with AES-256"
ProcedureStep-by-step how-to (SOPs, runbooks)"How to onboard a new employee"
GuidelineRecommended, not mandatoryBenchmarks, vendor implementation guides, reference architectures

Common policies: acceptable use (AUP), bring your own device (BYOD), clean desk, information security, incident response, data classification and retention, access control, data disposal, vulnerability disclosure and privacy. Business continuity and disaster recovery plans are governance documents too.

Audits and assessments

  • Internal: self-assessments and the audit committee. External: regulators and independent third-party auditors, which give more assurance.
  • A gap analysis compares where you are with where a framework says you should be.
  • An audit charter sets the scope and authority; frequency is set by policy or regulation.
  • Frameworks may be industry-based (PCI DSS for card payments), international (ISO 27001) or regional. Threat models such as MITRE ATT&CK, the Cyber Kill Chain and the Diamond Model serve as reference sources.

Security awareness

  • Training types: onboarding, ongoing, targeted (for a role, such as finance staff and invoice fraud) and corrective (after someone clicks a phishing test).
  • Topics: social engineering, business email compromise (BEC), passwords, removable media, remote and hybrid work, emerging threats such as deepfakes.
  • Measure it: phishing-simulation click and report rates, management reports, behaviour risk scores. Training that isn't measured can't be improved.

Check your understanding

"All passwords must be at least 14 characters" is an example of which document?

A standard sets the specific, measurable requirement that supports a policy.

Employees who click a simulated phishing email are automatically enrolled in a short follow-up course. What type of training is this?

Corrective training responds to a specific behaviour. Measuring click rates over time shows whether it works.

A company compares its current controls with ISO 27001 to see what it is missing before certification. What is this?

A gap analysis shows the difference between current controls and a framework's requirements.
