Lesson 7: Explain Resiliency and Site Security Concepts

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801)

Overview

Availability is a third of the CIA triad. This lesson covers how organizations keep running through hardware failures, disasters and attacks, and how buildings are protected.

You will learn:

  • redundancy: load balancing, clustering, high availability, autoscaling and power protection;
  • backups: retention, immutability and restoration testing;
  • hot, warm and cold recovery sites, business continuity and disaster recovery;
  • recovery metrics (RTO, RPO, MTTR, MTBF) and physical security.

On the exam this is mostly objective 3.4 (resilience and recovery), with physical threats from 2.3 and 2.5.

Step 2: Key ideas to remember

Redundancy

  • Load balancing spreads traffic across several servers; clustering lets servers take over from each other; autoscaling adds capacity when demand grows. Together they give high availability.
  • Power: a UPS (battery) bridges short outages and gives time to shut down cleanly; a generator runs for longer outages; redundant power supplies and surge protectors protect each device.
  • Platform diversity: different vendors or hardware so one flaw doesn't take everything down.

Backups that actually work

  • Immutable backups can't be changed or deleted for a set time, so ransomware can't encrypt them.
  • Keep at least one copy offsite or offline. Decide retention (how long) and scope (what is included).
  • Test restores regularly. A backup that has never been restored is only a hope.

Recovery sites

SiteReady to useCost
HotWithin minutes or hours: equipment and current data in placeHighest
WarmWithin days: equipment in place, data must be restoredMedium
ColdWeeks: space and power onlyLowest

Recovery metrics

MetricQuestion it answers
RTO (recovery time objective)How long can we be down?
RPO (recovery point objective)How much data can we afford to lose? (This sets how often to back up.)
MTTR (mean time to repair)How long does a fix usually take?
MTBF (mean time between failures)How long does it usually run before failing?

Business continuity keeps the business running during a disruption; disaster recovery restores IT systems afterwards. Both are tested with failover tests, simulations and tabletop exercises.

Site security

Fences, lighting, guards, badges and locks control entry. An access control vestibule (two doors, only one open at a time) stops tailgating, when someone follows an authorized person through a door. Cameras deter and record; card skimmers and shoulder surfing are physical attacks to watch for.

Check your understanding

The business can lose at most one hour of data. Which metric is this, and what does it decide?

The recovery point objective is acceptable data loss, so backups must run at least hourly. RTO is acceptable downtime.

Ransomware encrypted a company's servers and tried to delete its backups, but failed. What backup feature most likely saved them?

Immutable backups can't be changed or deleted during their retention period, even by an administrator account.

Which recovery site has equipment in place but needs data restored before use, and costs less than a fully ready site?

A warm site sits between a hot site (ready almost at once) and a cold site (space and power only).
