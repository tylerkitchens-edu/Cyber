Lesson 1: Summarize Fundamental Security Concepts

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801)

Overview

Level 5 prepares you for CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801, also called V8), the most widely requested entry-level security certification. Security+ asks you to recognise and explain: given a situation, which threat is it, which control fits, and why.

This first lesson sets out the vocabulary the rest of the course is built on.

You will learn:

  • the goals of security: confidentiality, integrity and availability (CIA), plus non-repudiation;
  • authentication, authorization and accounting (AAA), least privilege and Zero Trust;
  • how controls are grouped by category (technical, managerial, operational, physical) and by type (preventive, deterrent, detective, corrective, compensating, directive);
  • why change management (approvals, impact analysis, backout plans, maintenance windows) is a security process.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 1.1 (security concepts and controls) and 1.2 (change management).

Step 3: Key ideas to remember

The goals: CIA, plus non-repudiation

GoalMeansExample control
ConfidentialityOnly the right people can read itEncryption, access permissions
IntegrityIt has not been changed without anyone knowingHashing, digital signatures
AvailabilityIt works when people need itBackups, redundant servers
Non-repudiationSomeone can't deny they did itDigital signatures, audit logs

AAA, least privilege and Zero Trust

  • Authentication proves who you are; authorization decides what you may do; accounting records what you did.
  • Least privilege: give each person and program only the access the job needs, and nothing more.
  • Zero Trust: never trust a request just because it comes from inside the network. Check the user, the device and the request every time.
  • Defense in depth: several layers of controls, so one failure doesn't open everything.

Controls: category and type

Every control has a category (what it is) and a type (what it does). The exam often asks for one or the other.

CategoryExamples
Technical (logical)Firewalls, MFA, encryption
Managerial (administrative)Policies, risk assessments
OperationalSecurity guards, awareness training: things people do
Physical (environmental)Locks, fences, fire suppression
TypeWhat it doesExample
PreventiveStops it happeningA locked door, MFA
DeterrentDiscourages an attemptA warning sign, visible cameras
DetectiveNotices it happenedLog review, an intrusion detection alert
CorrectivePuts things rightRestoring from backup
CompensatingStands in when the normal control can't be usedIsolating an old system that can't be patched
DirectiveTells people what to doA policy that says "lock your screen"

Change management is security

Unplanned changes cause outages and open holes. A change advisory board (CAB) approves changes after an impact analysis; each change has an owner, test results, a backout plan (how to undo it) and a maintenance window. Afterwards, diagrams and procedures are updated and the configuration is kept under version control. Watch for technical side effects: restarts, downtime, legacy applications and dependencies.

Check your understanding

A company posts "This area is monitored by CCTV" at its entrance. What type of control is the sign?

The sign discourages an attempt. The cameras themselves, when someone reviews the footage, are detective.

A legacy system can't be patched, so it is moved to its own isolated network segment. What type of control is the segmentation?

The normal control (patching) can't be applied, so a different control makes up for it.

Before a firewall change, the team writes down exactly how to undo it if something breaks. What is this called?

The backout plan is the undo. The impact analysis is what the change might affect; the maintenance window is when it happens.
