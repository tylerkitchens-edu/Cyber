Lesson 10: Assess Endpoint Security Capabilities

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801)

Overview

Endpoints (laptops, desktops, servers, phones) are where users work and where most attacks land. This lesson covers hardening them, protecting them and managing them.

You will learn:

  • hardening: secure baselines, removing what isn't needed, Group Policy and SELinux;
  • antivirus, endpoint detection and response (EDR) and extended detection and response (XDR);
  • application allow lists and block lists, and host-based intrusion prevention;
  • mobile device management (MDM) and the asset life cycle, from purchase to secure disposal.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 4.1 (endpoint and operating system security, hardening, application control, MDM) and 4.2 (hardware, software and data asset management).

Step 3: Key ideas to remember

Hardening

  • Start from a secure baseline, such as a CIS Benchmark: a tested list of secure settings for each operating system.
  • Remove or disable what isn't needed: services, ports, default accounts, unused software.
  • Patch the operating system, firmware and applications on a schedule.
  • Enforce settings centrally: Group Policy on Windows; SELinux (mandatory access control) on Linux.
  • Encrypt disks, and turn on the host firewall.

Antivirus, EDR and XDR

ToolWhat it does
AntivirusBlocks known malware, mostly by signature
EDRRecords what happens on each endpoint (processes, files, connections), detects suspicious behaviour, and lets analysts investigate and isolate the device remotely
XDREDR extended across email, network, cloud and identity, so one attack is seen as one story

Application control

An allow list lets only approved programs run: very strong, but needs upkeep. A block list stops known-bad programs: easier, but misses anything new. Sandboxing runs untrusted code in an isolated space.

Mobile devices

MDM enforces a passcode and encryption, pushes apps and settings, separates work and personal data, and can locate or remotely wipe a lost phone. Policies differ for company-owned and bring-your-own (BYOD) devices.

Asset management

You can't protect what you don't know you have. Every device and licence is tracked through its life cycle: procurement, assignment to an owner, monitoring, and disposal, where drives are wiped or destroyed and a certificate of destruction is kept.

Check your understanding

Which tool lets an analyst see the full chain of processes on a laptop and isolate it from the network remotely?

EDR records endpoint activity and can contain a device. Traditional antivirus mainly blocks known malware.

A kiosk computer should only ever run one browser and one ticketing app. What is the strongest control?

An allow list blocks everything not approved, including malware nobody has seen yet.

Old laptops containing customer data are being retired. What must happen to them?

Disposal is part of the asset life cycle: sanitize or destroy the storage and keep evidence, such as a certificate of destruction.
