Lesson 16: Summarize Data Protection and Compliance Concepts

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801)

Overview

Data is usually what attackers want and what laws regulate. This lesson covers classifying data, protecting it in every state, and meeting legal and privacy obligations.

You will learn:

  • data types, states (at rest, in transit, in use) and classifications;
  • protection methods: encryption, hashing, masking, tokenization, de-identification, and data loss prevention (DLP);
  • data roles (owner, controller, processor, custodian, steward) and the data life cycle;
  • compliance: privacy rights, legal holds, consequences of non-compliance, and compliance monitoring.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 3.3 (protecting data) and 5.4 (security compliance).

Step 2: Key ideas to remember

States and classifications

  • Data is at rest (stored), in transit (moving across a network) or in use (being processed in memory). Each state needs its own protection.
  • Data can be structured (database tables) or unstructured (documents, email, images).
  • Business labels such as public, sensitive, confidential, restricted and critical; government labels such as secret and top secret. Classification decides how data must be handled, marked and labelled.
  • Regulated categories: personal information, health data, financial data, children's data, intellectual property and legal data.

Data loss prevention (DLP) spots sensitive data (card numbers, health records) leaving by email, upload or USB and blocks or reports it. Geofencing and controls on data location keep data in allowed places.

Who is responsible

  • Data owner: a senior person accountable for the data and its classification.
  • Data controller: decides why and how personal data is processed. Data processor (and subprocessor): processes it on the controller's behalf, such as a payroll provider.
  • Data custodian: the IT role that stores and protects it day to day. Data steward: looks after its quality and correct labelling.

The data life cycle runs from creation through management, distribution and retention to secure disposal.

Compliance

  • Privacy rights include the right to be forgotten, data correction, opting in or out, and limits on processing.
  • A legal hold overrides normal deletion: data relevant to a lawsuit must be kept.
  • Non-compliance can bring fines, sanctions, legal action, broken contracts, loss of licence and reputational damage.
  • Compliance monitoring uses attestations and acknowledgements (staff confirming they have read a policy); compliance training covers data handling, anti-bribery and anti-money laundering.

Check your understanding

A payment system replaces card numbers with random values and keeps the real numbers in a secure vault. What is this?

Tokenization swaps the real value for a meaningless token; only the vault can map it back.

A company hires a payroll firm to process employee data on its behalf. What is the payroll firm's role?

The company is the controller (decides why and how); the payroll firm processes on its behalf.

A lawsuit is filed, and IT is told not to delete any email from the people involved, whatever the retention policy says. What is this?

A legal hold preserves relevant data and overrides normal deletion schedules.
