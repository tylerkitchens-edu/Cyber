Lesson 15: Explain Risk Management Processes

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801)

Overview

No organization can remove every risk. Risk management is how it finds risks, measures them, decides what to do about each one, and keeps track, including the risks that come from suppliers.

You will learn:

  • risk identification, assessment and analysis: qualitative and quantitative (SLE, ARO, ALE);
  • the risk register, risk appetite and residual risk, and the business impact analysis;
  • risk treatment: mitigate, transfer, avoid or accept;
  • third-party risk: vendor selection, agreements (SLA, MOU, NDA, MSA, SOW), monitoring and penetration testing.

On the exam this is mostly objectives 5.2 (risk management) and 5.3 (third-party risk), with penetration testing from 5.5.

Step 2: Key ideas to remember

Measuring risk

Qualitative analysis rates likelihood and impact as low, medium or high. Quantitative analysis uses money:

TermMeaningExample
SLE (single loss expectancy)Cost of one occurrence = asset value × exposure factor$100,000 server × 40% damage = $40,000
ARO (annualized rate of occurrence)How often per year it is expectedOnce every 4 years = 0.25
ALE (annualized loss expectancy)Expected cost per year = SLE × ARO$40,000 × 0.25 = $10,000

If a control costs less per year than the ALE it removes, it is usually worth buying.

Tracking and deciding

  • The risk register lists every risk with its owner, score, current mitigations and treatment, and is reviewed regularly.
  • A business impact analysis (BIA) works out which processes matter most and what an outage costs. It sets the RTO and RPO from Lesson 7.
  • Risk appetite is how much risk leadership will accept; residual risk is what remains after controls.

Four treatments

Mitigate (add controls), transfer (cyber insurance, a contract), avoid (stop the risky activity), accept (leadership signs off, and it is recorded and reviewed).

Third-party risk

  • Selecting a vendor: requests for information, proposals and quotes (RFI, RFP, RFQ), due diligence, and checks for conflicts of interest.
  • Agreements: an SLA sets measurable service levels (an SLO is one target within it); an MOU or MOA records an intent to cooperate; an NDA protects confidential information; an MSA sets the overall terms and each SOW defines one piece of work.
  • Monitoring: a right-to-audit clause, service-level monitoring, vendor assessments and compliance attestations.

Penetration testing

A pen test is an authorized simulated attack. In a known environment (white box) testers get full details; in a partially known (grey box) some; in an unknown environment (black box) none. Reconnaissance is passive (public information only) or active (touching the target). The rules of engagement set what is allowed, when and where.

Check your understanding

A server worth $50,000 would be 50% damaged by a flood, expected once every 10 years. What is the ALE?

SLE = $50,000 × 0.5 = $25,000. ARO = 0.1. ALE = $25,000 × 0.1 = $2,500.

Which document sets the measurable uptime and response times a vendor promises?

A service-level agreement sets measurable service levels, often with penalties if they are missed.

Pen testers are given nothing but the company name. What kind of test is this?

An unknown environment (black box) test is closest to an outside attacker's view.
